# Progress Reporting

Read this reference after completing a roadmap item or when the learner asks how far they have progressed against the timetable.

## Completion event

Produce a progress snapshot when an item gains completion-level evidence, normally **Demonstrated** or **Retained**, or when a build/setup item meets its explicit completion criterion. Do not claim a completed step for explanation, reading, or guided practice alone. For an in-progress or provisional outcome, show what remains without increasing the completed count.

## Calculate progress

Use the roadmap and dated reports as the source of truth. Report:

- the item just completed and its evidence level;
- completed versus total items in the current phase, separated into `Learn`, `Build`, and `Exit criteria` when present;
- total demonstrated or retained roadmap outcomes when the roadmap tracks that measure;
- the number and date range of supporting reports; and
- the next incomplete prerequisite or milestone.

Do not combine unequal checkboxes into a precise percentage unless the roadmap defines weights. Counts are acceptable and clearer. Distinguish historical completion from retention: a previously demonstrated item remains completed, while a later failed retention check is reported as a current gap rather than silently erasing history.

## Compare with the timetable

Prefer explicit planned dates and weekly targets. If only a start date and relative phase weeks exist, derive the current roadmap week from elapsed calendar time and label it as a schedule estimate. Compare the current phase and completed evidence with the planned phase or weekly focus.

Use one of these labels:

- **Ahead:** required evidence planned for a future checkpoint is already complete.
- **On track:** current evidence satisfies the targets due by now and required prerequisites are not blocked.
- **Behind:** one or more explicitly due targets lack sufficient evidence.
- **Replanned:** the roadmap records an intentional schedule change.
- **Unknown:** dates, milestones, or expected weekly outputs are insufficient for a defensible comparison.

Being inside the planned phase window does not by itself prove `On track`. If weekly rows list only broad themes rather than measurable due outcomes, say that the learner is within the planned window but exact ahead/behind status is unknown. Do not equate fast checkbox completion with mastery or describe pace as good without citing evidence quality.

## Interpret the evidence

Summarize how far the learner has come using observable facts from reports:

- independently demonstrated concepts or artifacts;
- corrections that were successfully reassessed;
- active gaps and retention candidates;
- whether progress is conceptual, practical, or both; and
- the most important work still required for the current phase.

Use plain language. A useful interpretation might say that conceptual foundations are progressing strongly while practical build evidence has not started. Avoid generic praise and unsupported predictions.

## Persist and present

After a completion event:

1. Update the roadmap's current progress counts and schedule status.
2. Update the appropriate weekly row with actual outcome, evidence link, problem or correction, and next action.
3. Add `## Progress against plan` to the dated session report.
4. Show the same essential snapshot in the closing response so the learner need not open the files to understand their position.

Use this compact presentation:

```text
Completed: <item and evidence level>
Phase progress: Learn <x/y>; Build <x/y>; Exit <x/y>
Schedule: <label and evidence/uncertainty>
Evidence trend: <what reports demonstrate, including remaining gaps>
Next checkpoint: <one required action>
```

Preserve historical reports. Do not rewrite older reports merely to add a newer reporting format; add the snapshot to the current completion report and use the roadmap for the latest cumulative position.
